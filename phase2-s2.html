The customer problem
Now that you understand how ad platforms work, you're ready to understand why they break. This section is about the problem Journify was built to solve — from the customer's perspective, in the customer's language.
Day 11–12 3 tasks
Why this order matters
Problem before solution. Always. You've just learned how platforms make decisions — now learn why those decisions go wrong. By the end of this section you should be able to explain signal loss to a performance marketer who has never heard the term. Not as a technical concept. As something they have probably already experienced without knowing the name for it.
Understand signal loss — deeply
~1 hour · Read + reflect
Go back to the How to speak Journify glossary from Phase 1 — this time, read it differently. You now know how platforms work. Read the problem terms through that lens: signal loss, browser pixel, iOS ATT, match rate. Connect what you learned in Section 1 to why those things break.
A conversion signal is what happens when someone buys something and the ad platform finds out. iOS 14 changed the rules. Pixels started missing 30–40% of purchases. The platforms kept optimising — but from an incomplete picture. That is the problem.

Read the competitive guide — strategic frame only
~20 min · First section only
The competitive guide explains how every alternative a customer might consider fits into one of four categories — and what none of them actually fix. Read the strategic frame section only at this stage. Do not read the full guide yet. The goal is to understand the positioning, not memorise the battle cards.
Customers will arrive with existing tools — MMPs, CDPs, agency-managed pixels. Your job at this stage is to understand why none of those solve the signal quality problem Journify fixes.
Watch a Grain call — prospect who didn't know signal loss
~30–45 min
Ask your manager for a specific call where the prospect didn't understand signal loss at the start of the conversation. Watch how the concept gets introduced, how confusion gets resolved, and what language actually lands. Notice what the customer says before they understand — that's what you'll hear in your own calls.
The goal isn't to memorise the script. It's to see the moment when a customer recognises their own pain in what's being explained. That's the moment you're learning to create.
